**An NSOP operator is a company that holds a Non-Scheduled Operator Permit from the DGCA, India's aviation regulator. Only NSOP holders may fly you on a charter flight for a fee — so the first question about any wedding flight is: who is the operator, and is their permit valid?**

## What the permit means

Airlines fly scheduled routes to a published timetable. Charter flights are different: the aircraft goes where and when you need it. In India, a company that flies charters for paying passengers needs a **Non-Scheduled Operator Permit (NSOP)** from the Directorate General of Civil Aviation. The permit lists the aircraft the operator may use for commercial flights.

A private owner's jet without an NSOP may not be hired out for money. If someone offers a flight on "a friend's jet", ask whose permit it flies under.

## What the DGCA asks operators to publish

The DGCA asks operators to publish information such as the age of their aircraft, maintenance history and pilot experience on their websites, and it publishes safety rankings of NSOP operators <span class="verify">from time to time</span>. These are useful starting points for your own checks.

## The checks we make before any quote

1. **The operator's permit** — valid, and covering the aircraft in your quote.
2. **The aircraft itself** — registration and airworthiness papers for that exact aircraft.
3. **Insurance** — passenger and aircraft insurance in date for your flight.
4. **For helicopters, landing permissions** — the operator's landing request, the venue owner's NOC and the district's nod, all in place before you pay in full.

## Questions to ask any charter seller

- Which operator will fly us, and what is its NSOP number?
- Which exact aircraft (type and registration)?
- Is the insurance in date for our flight date?
- Who do we pay — the company, or a person? (Always the company.)
